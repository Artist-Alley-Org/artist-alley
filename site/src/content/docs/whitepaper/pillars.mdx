title: Design pillars
description: The principles we use to make product decisions.

Artist Alley is built around two product pillars. When a design decision is ambiguous, we resolve it by asking which pillar the choice serves best.

## 1. Artist-first, not archive-first

Artists upload and forget. Metadata happens automatically where possible. Friction is the enemy of an archive that gets used.

- File-format defaults: dragging a folder Just Works.
- EXIF, dimensions, color profile, embedded thumbnails parsed on ingest.
- Optional auto-tagging via image embeddings (pgvector).
- Required fields kept to an absolute minimum — admin-extensible per studio.
- Any common action — upload, find, comment, share, approve — reachable in three clicks or fewer. When a workflow takes more than three clicks, the design is wrong, not the user.

**UX target:** [ArtStation](https://www.artstation.com/) levels of simplicity for upload. If a studio wants more metadata, an admin adds the fields. The default is that artists get out of the upload modal in seconds.

## 2. Review-grade

Reviewers come back to Artist Alley every day. The tool is shaped for that loop.

- Tracks unreviewed assets since your last session.
- Remembers your spot — leave mid-batch, resume later.
- Async commenting per asset (Frame.io-style) with timestamp + frame anchors.
- Live presenter sessions over SSE for sync review meetings.
- Dedicated review canvas with zoom / pan / tile / fullscreen.
- Animator-grade video transport (JKL, frame-step, I/O loop region).

The review tool is the centre of gravity. The rest of the product exists so reviewers have something to review.

## Underneath the pillars: posture

The pillars describe how the product behaves. Three postures describe how it lives in the world:

- **End-to-end, not glue-code.** A studio shouldn't have to stitch together a DAM, a review tool, a transfer service, a chat bot, and a wiki to do its job. Artist Alley aims to be the one self-hostable system for upload → review → version → archive → ship.
- **Open by default.** The core is open source. Every feature on the roadmap lives in the AGPL binary an operator can self-host forever. Premium capabilities are *additive*, gated only when an operator is using us to monetize third parties or when we're carrying real operational burden on their behalf.
- **Federated, no SaaS rent.** Studios working with external partners — co-development teams, marketing agencies, licensing partners, museums on loan — share assets directly between independently-operated instances. No middle vendor, no per-outsider seat licensing, no metadata-stripping export-then-import. The protocol is [ArchivePub](/protocol/archivepub/) — an open, ActivityPub-shaped spec for DAM federation that any other DAM can implement. We built it because nobody else has, and because federation between studios should be a property of the protocol, not a feature you rent.

## Why these three postures together matter

Each posture, alone, has been done before. End-to-end DAMs exist (commercial). Open-source DAMs exist (PHP, archivist-shaped). Federated content platforms exist (Mastodon, Pixelfed, PeerTube — for messaging, photos, video, respectively). What hasn't existed: all three at once, on a single self-hostable binary built this decade rather than the last one. That combination is the durable position. The artist-first pillar is what makes daily users come back; the review-grade pillar is what makes studio leads sign off; the three postures together are why studios can adopt without strategic regret.
